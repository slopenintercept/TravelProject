Design notes — draft 1

Three directions in the spirit of the Mindtrip reference, each shown on the same three screens with content copied from a real saved trip (Japan, 7 days, Alex and friend2). Nothing here is wired to the app. The Picks buttons are clickable so you can feel the states.

What was taken from the reference: very large, tightly tracked geometric headlines; black-and-white base; fully rounded controls; big rounded panels in deep navy, rust and moss with pale text; translucent chips sitting on those panels. What was not taken: its logo, wordmark, typeface (Circular is licensed; these use Figtree, IBM Plex and Bricolage Grotesque), photography and page layouts.

A · Postcard — closest to the reference

  • Same steps as today, new skin and new layouts.
  • Start is one sentence you fill in, not a form grid.
  • Picks are tiles that change colour when marked; a floating bar keeps the count and Save.
  • Review is one coloured panel per category, with the price as the loudest thing in it.

B · Ledger — the precise tool

  • Three fixed panes: steps, travelers and agents on the left; the work in the middle; budget and conflicts always visible on the right.
  • Every number is monospaced and right-aligned. Flight alternatives are rows you select in place, not a Swap menu.
  • Budget shows spent against each category's allotment. The app computes this today but only reveals it in a hover tooltip.

C · Night flight — the wildcard, and a flow change

  • One screen for the whole trip. Left: a feed where agents report and ask for your next decision. Right: the plan, filling in as you answer.
  • Review and Itinerary merge: conflicts become cards with actions, and the calendar is always beside them.
  • The draft plan sits beside Picks, so marking a place visibly changes the days.
  • Includes a map, which the app does not have today. Ideas already carry coordinates, so it is buildable; it is drawn as a labelled placeholder.

Not mocked yet

Date choice, the join and who-are-you screens, the waiting screens while agents work, choosing flights and stays, the Itinerary tab in A and B, and Finish.

Open questions

  • Photos. The reference leans on photography and Tripwright has none. All three directions work without it. Adding place photos needs an image source.
  • The map in C is new scope.
  • C's "Swap it in" and "Drop" on a conflict are new actions built from ones that exist in the Itinerary tab.
