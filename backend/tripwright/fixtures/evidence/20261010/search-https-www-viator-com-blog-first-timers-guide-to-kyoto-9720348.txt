Many first-time visitors to Kyoto have UNESCO World Heritage–listed Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion, high on their itineraries: what’s not to love about a 14th-century gold-leaf-covered Zen temple picturesquely situated beside a pond reflecting the golden gleam? # Visiting Kyoto For the First Time? Here’s What to See and Do

The serpentine layout of the Fushimi Inari Shrine in Kyoto, Japan.

Kyoto embodies what many people think of when they imagine traditional Japan: red shrine gates, shady bamboo groves, hillsides blazing red and orange in fall, and elegant geisha shuffling along lantern-lit backstreets. It was Japan’s capital for more than 1,000 years before the government moved to Tokyo in 1868, and still exudes a quiet grandeur.
