### What time does Meiji Shrine open and is it free?

  

The shrine opens at sunrise and closes around sunset, so hours shift with the seasons (opening runs roughly 5:00–6:40 a.m. through the year). The grounds and main hall are free; only the Inner Garden charges a small entry fee.

 

### How long do you need at Meiji Shrine? [...] Go early: Meiji Shrine opens at sunrise (roughly 5:00–6:40 a.m. depending on the month), and the first two hours are when the 100,000-tree forest is at its quietest. Entry is free. The etiquette essentials fit in a minute: bow once before passing under the torii gate, walk to either side of the path (the center is reserved for the deity), purify your hands at the water basin, and pray with two bows, two claps, one bow. Dress is casual-fine — respect here is about behavior, not clothing. [...] Meiji Shrine sits inside a forest of roughly 100,000 trees, planted a century ago and designed to feel ancient. At 8 a.m. on a weekday, the gravel path is mostly yours: you hear your own footsteps, the crows, and not much else — a genuinely startling silence given that Harajuku’s crowds are five minutes away. By late morning, especially on weekends, the same path carries a steady stream of visitors and the spell weakens.
